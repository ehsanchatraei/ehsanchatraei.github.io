Research

How can remote sensing help us measure what matters in a field? Three projects follow that question from a potato plant to a satellite image.

Carberry, Manitoba · Multispectral imagery and LiDAR

At row closure, the canopy gives two different answers.

In the 2023–2024 trials, a chlorophyll-sensitive index had the strongest relationship with petiole nitrate among the vegetation indices tested. A subsequent experiment used multispectral imagery and LiDAR from the 2024–2025 seasons to test what canopy colour and structure could reveal together.

Visible to shortwave infrared spectroscopy

The same crop looks different in the field and in the lab.

We compared measurements taken over the canopy with measurements from fresh leaves and dried tissue. Fresh leaves gave the strongest nitrogen estimates. Aligning the canopy spectra to the fresh-leaf reference improved the field model, showing how much the measurement conditions influence the signal.

Open potato spectroscopy data

Explore the 2024 Carberry measurements. The archive contains canopy, fresh-leaf and dried-tissue spectra linked to plot-level petiole nitrate, with metadata and documentation for reuse.

Digital soil mapping

Combining satellite views to map soil carbon.

In cropland in South Moravia, Czech Republic, our team combined PlanetScope SuperDove imagery with selected Sentinel-2 bands and terrain data. The strongest model for soil organic carbon used the fused imagery with terrain and reached an R² of 0.83 in the study. Read the Catena paper ↗

Hyperspectral soil mapping in Manitoba

As co-PI on a Mitacs Accelerate project with Planet Labs, I helped develop a study that pairs Tanager satellite imagery with field and laboratory spectra to evaluate how well satellite imaging can characterize agricultural soils.
